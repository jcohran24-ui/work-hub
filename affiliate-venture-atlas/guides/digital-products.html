How to sell your first digital product without overbuilding it.

You do not need a library of products or an expensive website. You need a clearly defined buyer, a problem worth solving, a useful first version, and a trustworthy way to deliver it.

In this guide1. Find a specific problem2. Package a small solution3. Pick a checkout platform4. Price with real costs5. Launch and learn

1. Find a specific problem

Instead of starting with “I want to sell ebooks,” start with “Who has a problem I can solve with a useful file?” Consider planners, trackers, templates, mini-lessons, calculators and checklists. Look for questions real people repeatedly ask in niche communities, forums or search results.

Write your offer as: “I help [specific person] accomplish [specific outcome] with [specific format].” For example, “I help new pet sitters organize their first client bookings with a printable intake pack.” This is a positioning example, not proof of market demand.

Before building, talk with potential users, examine alternative products and check whether people currently pay for comparable solutions. Avoid copying another creator's work, protected branding or licensed material.

2. Build the smallest useful version

Choose one job your product must perform. If you are making a tracker, build one polished working spreadsheet rather than ten unfinished ones. If you are making a guide, show steps, examples and clear outcomes. Consider accessibility and make sure downloaded files open in common software.

  • Create the core deliverable and an honest sample page.
  • Test the download, editing and printing experience on a phone and computer.
  • Make a short instruction sheet explaining what is included and who it helps.
  • Write a refund and support policy appropriate for your selling platform.

3. Choose a simple selling platform

Gumroad is oriented toward creator products and digital delivery. systeme.io combines funnels, email and digital offers, making it worth evaluating if you plan to grow an email list. Shopify may fit if you need a fuller catalog and store experience.

Compare each provider's current fees, digital-delivery features, tax handling, refund workflow, file-size limits and customer support. Never decide solely by an advertised starter price: transaction fees and additional apps can change your real cost.

4. Price for value and costs

List platform fees, payment charges, optional software subscriptions, ad expenses and your support time. Then compare the value of the outcome with credible alternatives. A low price does not automatically increase sales, and a high price needs corresponding value and trust.

Example, not a prediction

For a $29 digital product with 10% in total assumed per-sale fees, you retain about $26.10 before fixed costs, refunds, taxes and your time. If monthly fixed and advertising costs are $95, you need 4 sales to cover those specified costs. Your actual fees and results will differ.

5. Launch small and measure honestly

Write one helpful tutorial related to your product, share the free sample where the community allows promotion, and invite feedback. Watch meaningful actions: page visits, free sample downloads, genuine buyer questions, and completed purchases. Do not confuse page views with product-market fit.

Revise the product after actual customer feedback, not just personal preference. Add upsells and automations only when they address a proven need. Keep clear customer-facing policies and avoid income promises.

Your first-week action plan

  1. Choose one audience and write three problems it regularly experiences.
  2. Select one problem and sketch a one-page solution.
  3. Build the first useful version.
  4. Ask at least three relevant people for specific usability feedback.
  5. Publish one honest product page and test its full purchase/download journey.

Editorial note: This is a general guide, not a personal product test, guarantee of sales, or legal/tax advice. Product features and fees change; verify them on each official site.
